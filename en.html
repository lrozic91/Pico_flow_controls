Pico Flow Controls

A Zagreb engineering and manufacturing company for the gas and oil industry. It was formed by merging three companies: Feromihin (founded 1992), Macel plin (2002) and PFC (2014). In 2025, its first year as one company, it had €39.4m in revenue.22116

1. Manufacturing

Own devices and pipeline equipment
  • eLBC: an electronic device that closes the valve when a pipeline breaks, also in a solar version. “1000 eLBC installed”.2250
  • RIKI: a calibration device for LBC units.23
  • Printed circuit boards: two SMT lines, 71,600 components per hour in total; also for outside customers.24
  • Pressure equipment: filters, separators, vessels and heat exchangers (ASME VIII, EN 13445, PED).26
  • AEGIS: plastic mesh for pipe protection, geogrids and fencing, made entirely in Croatia; shown at K 2025.2567
  • Pipe segments and steel structures (EN 13480, EN 1090-2), CNC machining and control cabinets (DCS/ESD).66

2. Gas solutions

Custom plants, from design to commissioning
  • KPPP: a mobile recompression unit that pumps gas out of a pipeline section instead of venting it; inlet up to 250 bar, up to 9,000 Sm³/h, 400 kW engine.29
  • Virtual pipeline: compressed gas containers (MEGC) on semi-trailers, 5,700 Nm³ per container; 14 delivered.30
  • Measuring and reducing stations: 30 stations, up to 250 bar.31
  • CNG, LPG, LNG and H2: six CNG stations and one self-service station.32
  • GRIZO synthetic gas stations, gas dehydration (5,000–100,000 Sm³/h), odorisation and analysers (15 chromatographs).653433

3. Field services

Testing, repairs and pipeline maintenance
  • Valve testing up to 650 bar (API 6A, 6D, 598) and safety valves up to 400 bar as an accredited inspection body.3536
  • Ballooning and hot tapping: work on a pipeline without interrupting supply, DN 80–200.38
  • Corrosion protection: exclusive Stopaq distributor for Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Slovenia, Serbia, Montenegro and Kosovo.37
  • Pipeline inspections (ILI and non-ILI), electrical works, process instrumentation and civil works.64

4. Software

Digital products for pipeline management
  • AYMO: a pipeline integrity management system with seven modules. Developed with Async Labs and shown in Kazakhstan, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Brazil and Berlin.275149
  • AMS: an asset management system; 46,013 assets entered and 3,857 tasks, also used by the LNG terminal.28

Blockchain

Pilot: PFC's business on a private chain

Orders, services, alarms, invoices and payments get a record nobody can change afterwards. Each party sees only what it is allowed to, and the data stays with PFC. The proposal is my conclusion, not the company's plan.

Why blockchain: today and with a chain

Benefits for PFC and its customers; the assessment is my conclusion

One source of truth

Todayreports in AMS, PDFs by e-mail, Excel at the customer; everyone has their own version.

With a chainPFC, the customer and the auditor read the same record, with the same time and fingerprint.

Proof instead of argument

Todaydisputes over whether a service was done, when an alarm happened and what amount was agreed.

With a chaintime and fingerprint are written once and cannot be changed later; any change shows at once.

Transparent but private

Todayyou either share the whole document or nothing.

With a chaineach role sees exactly its own: the customer its stations, the regulator alarms, the auditor amounts, competitors nothing.7268

Faster audit and control

Todaythe auditor samples some documents by hand, and controlling assembles reports from several systems.

With a chainchecking every record takes seconds; controlling sees each job's status from order to payment.

Rules in code

Todayrules (“invoice only after service”) exist on paper and depend on people.

With a chainthe smart contract enforces them: no invoice before a confirmed service, and only the customer confirms payment.

Market advantage

Todaya bid competes on price and references.

With a chainPFC offers a verifiable digital record for every station and device; a bank can finance confirmed invoices faster (my conclusion).

What ZKsync Prividium is

  • A private blockchain that ZKsync launched on 27 May 2025. The first partner was Memento, a chain built with Deutsche Bank.69
  • Transactions, counterparties and balances stay private, inside the company's infrastructure. Only ZK proofs that everything is correct go to Ethereum.68
  • The company decides who may see and call what, down to individual smart contract functions. An auditor or regulator can get read-only access.7271
  • Smart contracts are written in Solidity with the usual tools (Hardhat, Foundry, Viem).73
  • The company can run it itself or have Matter Labs run it as a service. Pricing is not published.68

Who uses it

  • Deutsche Bank / Memento: a private L2 for issuing and servicing tokenised funds. It grew out of Project DAMA under Singapore's MAS Project Guardian.6970
  • Launching a fund went from 2–3 months to 2–3 weeks.70
  • ZKsync also lists other banks and funds, but these are its own claims.68
  • I found no industrial company using Prividium. PFC would be among the first outside finance (my conclusion).

The PFCLedger smart contract

The full code that goes to the testnet; download .sol

// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
pragma solidity ^0.8.24;

/// @title PFC Ledger (demo)
/// @notice Simulates Pico Flow Controls' service business on a public ZKsync testnet:
///         order -> service on site -> eLBC alarm -> invoice -> payment.
///         Only ids, amounts and document fingerprints (hashes) go on chain;
///         the documents themselves stay in the company's systems.
/// @dev Demo only. On a private Prividium chain, access to these functions would
///      come from Prividium roles instead of the `roles` mapping.
contract PFCLedger {
    enum Role { None, PFC, Technician, Customer, Auditor }
    enum Status { None, Ordered, Serviced, Invoiced, Paid }

    struct Order {
        bytes32 station;
        address customer;
        uint64 amountCents;
        Status status;
    }

    address public immutable owner;
    mapping(address => Role) public roles;
    mapping(uint256 => Order) public orders;
    uint256 public orderCount;

    event RoleSet(address indexed who, Role role);
    event OrderCreated(uint256 indexed id, bytes32 indexed station, address indexed customer, bytes32 doc);
    event ServiceLogged(uint256 indexed id, bytes32 indexed station, address technician, bytes32 doc);
    event AlarmLogged(bytes32 indexed station, uint8 code, bytes32 doc);
    event InvoiceIssued(uint256 indexed id, uint64 amountCents, bytes32 doc);
    event PaymentConfirmed(uint256 indexed id, address customer);

    modifier only(Role r) {
        require(msg.sender == owner || roles[msg.sender] == r, "role");
        _;
    }

    constructor() {
        owner = msg.sender;
        roles[msg.sender] = Role.PFC;
        emit RoleSet(msg.sender, Role.PFC);
    }

    function setRole(address who, Role r) external {
        require(msg.sender == owner, "owner");
        roles[who] = r;
        emit RoleSet(who, r);
    }

    function createOrder(bytes32 station, address customer, bytes32 doc) external only(Role.PFC) returns (uint256 id) {
        id = ++orderCount;
        orders[id] = Order(station, customer, 0, Status.Ordered);
        emit OrderCreated(id, station, customer, doc);
    }

    function logService(uint256 id, bytes32 doc) external only(Role.Technician) {
        Order storage o = orders[id];
        require(o.status == Status.Ordered, "status");
        o.status = Status.Serviced;
        emit ServiceLogged(id, o.station, msg.sender, doc);
    }

    function logAlarm(bytes32 station, uint8 code, bytes32 doc) external only(Role.Technician) {
        emit AlarmLogged(station, code, doc);
    }

    function issueInvoice(uint256 id, uint64 amountCents, bytes32 doc) external only(Role.PFC) {
        Order storage o = orders[id];
        require(o.status == Status.Serviced, "status");
        o.status = Status.Invoiced;
        o.amountCents = amountCents;
        emit InvoiceIssued(id, amountCents, doc);
    }

    function confirmPayment(uint256 id) external {
        Order storage o = orders[id];
        require(o.status == Status.Invoiced && msg.sender == o.customer, "customer");
        o.status = Status.Paid;
        emit PaymentConfirmed(id, msg.sender);
    }
}

Why PFC

  • PFC already has the data: AMS tracks 46,013 assets and the LNG terminal uses it too, and about 1,000 eLBC units are installed.2822
  • It has its own IT and software (AYMO, AMS), so the pilot does not depend on an outside agency.2728
  • The customer gets a record PFC cannot change afterwards without it showing. That is an extra argument in tenders (my conclusion).
  • The same chain can later carry equipment records and certificates (my conclusion).

Risks and open questions

  • Production needs a commercial agreement with Matter Labs. Local development needs access to their private Docker registry.7174
  • For now a single sequencer runs the chain; decentralisation is only on the roadmap.68
  • Customers are mostly state energy companies. The pilot only makes sense if at least one customer wants to read the records (my conclusion).
  • Only fingerprints and made-up data may go on the public testnet, because it is public.75

Pilot plan

Durations are my estimate, about 4–5 months in total

0 · 2 weeksPick 1–3 stations and one customer, list the AMS data, request access from Matter Labs
1 · 3–4 weeksLocal Prividium in Docker, PFCLedger contract, roles PFC / customer / regulator / auditor74
2 · 2–3 monthsField pilot: the chosen stations' jobs go on the chain, the customer reads them and confirms payments
3 · 2 weeksReview: benefits, cost, decision on production and licence
